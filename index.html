The lemons problem

A catalogue that admits tools on self-asserted quality is worthless: anyone can claim their tool is good, so junk and gold list side by side and the price collapses. The fix is one rule — a tool lists only if its repository passes a benchmark, and the pass is a receipt anyone can reproduce.

Why it cannot be forged

No trusted signer

Nobody's signature makes a proof valid. The benchmark is deterministic over the repository, so anyone can re-run it and confirm the hash themselves.

The receipt carries the verdict

A proof holds the benchmark's own content-addressed verdict hash. You cannot assert a pass your code does not produce.

Re-verified, not remembered

verify() re-runs the benchmark against the repo. A listing is a live claim, not a badge earned once.

No manufactured scarcity

Passing is not rationed and not curated. Everything that passes lists; everything that does not, does not.
